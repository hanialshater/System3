# Jobs-to-be-done: functional, social, emotional

**evidence: sourced**

## The evidence

Fashion purchases satisfy functional (cover/protect), social (fit in or stand out in a specific context), and emotional (feel confident) jobs simultaneously — and online and offline channels satisfy these differently (offline may better serve tactile/emotional confidence jobs; online may better serve breadth-of-choice and price-comparison jobs). A recommendation experience that only addresses the functional job while ignoring the social/emotional context (an interview, a wedding, a vacation) misses most of the actual motivation.

Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0969698922003149

## Why this file exists

This isn't a need in the same sense as the other files in this directory — it's a checklist you run *against* whatever need you've already inferred, to catch a blind spot. A customer session that looks purely functional on the surface (browsing jackets) may actually be driven by a social job (a specific event) or an emotional job (feeling confident in an unfamiliar situation) that changes what commentary should actually say.

## How to use it

For any inferred need, ask: is this actually functional, social, or emotional — or some mix? Commentary that answers only the functional layer ("this is a warm jacket") when the real job is social ("will I look right at this event") reads as tone-deaf even when factually accurate.
