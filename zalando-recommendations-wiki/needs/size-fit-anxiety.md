# Size and fit anxiety

**evidence: sourced**

## The evidence

- Fit/sizing issues drive roughly **50-67% of fashion e-commerce returns**; "too small" alone is the single most common complaint (~34% of all returns). Overall fashion return rates run 20-40%, up to 75% for some categories/brands — far higher than general e-commerce's ~20%. — https://koozee.ai/blog/fashion-ecommerce-return-rate
- **90% of apparel sites fail** to let users adequately judge look, size, or fit before buying; 80%+ under-provide sizing information (Baymard Institute, 1,765 research hours, 18 sites, 370 sessions). — https://baymard.com/blog/apparel-ecommerce-ux-research-launch
- **Bracketing** (ordering multiple sizes/colors, intending to return most) is now mainstream, up sharply from ~40% in 2018 — a customer-side coping mechanism for a need the product experience doesn't solve directly. — https://eightx.co/blog/average-ecommerce-return-rate
- Baymard recommends an **aggregate fit subscore** (runs small / true to size / runs large) distilled from reviews, separate from the general star rating — the specific evidence fit-anxious shoppers are hunting for and often can't find. — https://baymard.com/blog/apparel-provide-aggregate-fit-subscore-in-reviews
- Apparel images need **on-model, in-context views** — attributes like drape and rise are nearly impossible to judge from flat product photography, and this drives both purchase hesitation and post-purchase returns when reality doesn't match expectation. — https://baymard.com/blog/human-model

## Signals that suggest this need

Size chart opened (especially more than once), no add-to-bag after size chart interaction, unfamiliar brand, extended/plus-size context, returns history on similarly-fitted items.
