# Filter fatigue and choice overload

**evidence: sourced**

## The evidence

Sites with weak filtering usability see **67-90% abandonment** among users seeking a specific product type, vs. 17-33% on sites with strong filter/sort tooling. 38% of e-commerce sites don't have filters for all the product info they actually display, and 54% over-categorize — treating an attribute like color or fit as a top-level category instead of a filter — forcing shoppers into dead-end browsing paths.

Source: https://baymard.com/research/ecommerce-product-lists

## Signals that suggest this need

Filters applied then abandoned without a resulting click, fast scrolling with no item opens on a large category page, repeated filter changes without progress toward a decision.

## How this differs from comparison friction

This is about *too many undifferentiated options* with no way to narrow, not a *small specific set* the customer is stuck choosing between. See [comparison-friction.md](comparison-friction.md) for the latter, and don't conflate the two responses.

## Response

[../patterns/for-decision-paralysis-do-curate-not-expand.md](../patterns/for-decision-paralysis-do-curate-not-expand.md) — narrow the assortment or fix the filtering, don't add more recommendation modules on top of an already-overloaded page.
