# Discovery vs. targeted search

**evidence: sourced**

## The evidence

Fashion shopping is frequently **exploratory**, not query-driven: customers arrive without a specific product in mind, searching by aesthetic ("quiet luxury," "coastal grandmother") rather than by attribute. Product discovery through browsing, editorial content, and curated collections captures demand that keyword search structurally cannot, because the shopper doesn't yet know what to type. Sites optimized only for query-driven search under-serve this half of fashion shopping.

Source: https://algonomy.com/blogs/ecommerce-product-discovery-optimization-fashion-retailers/

A related, specific gap: occasion-based need (wedding guest, interview, vacation) is served today mainly through pre-curated editorial collections rather than structured occasion filtering — fine when the occasion matches an existing collection, a real gap when it doesn't (e.g. "beach wedding, humid, need to travel-pack it").

Source: https://www.theknot.com/content/where-to-buy-wedding-guest-dresses

## Signals that suggest which mission this is

**Targeted search**: arrived via a specific search query or a direct product link, narrow browse pattern, fast path to a small set of items. **Discovery**: broad, unstructured browsing, searching by vague/aesthetic terms, no fixed target, exploring multiple categories.

## Response

For discovery missions, lean on editorial/curated surfaces and the style vocabulary in [../styles/modern-aesthetic-tags.md](../styles/modern-aesthetic-tags.md) rather than forcing the customer into attribute filters. For occasion-specific discovery, check actual collection match — [../patterns/for-occasion-need-do-check-collection-match.md](../patterns/for-occasion-need-do-check-collection-match.md).
