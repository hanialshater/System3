# Comparison friction

**evidence: sourced**

## The evidence

Classic choice-overload research (the Iyengar/Lepper jam study, replicated in retail contexts) shows large assortments attract more browsers but convert far worse than curated small sets — a 24-item display drew more visitors but converted at only 3%, vs. 30% for a 6-item display. A study of 1.6 million consumers attributed 64% of purchase-probability decline to shoppers avoiding search/comparison entirely when facing too many near-identical options.

Source: https://www.crobox.com/blog/choice-overload

## Signals that suggest this need

Repeated switching between a small, specific set of products (typically 2-3), declining time-on-page across repeat visits to the same items (a sign of "already knows the content, still stuck"), one item saved but not purchased while another stays open.

## How this differs from filter fatigue

Comparison friction is about a *small, specific set* the customer can't resolve between. [filter-fatigue-and-choice-overload.md](filter-fatigue-and-choice-overload.md) is about *too many undifferentiated options* with no way to narrow. They call for different responses — don't conflate them.
