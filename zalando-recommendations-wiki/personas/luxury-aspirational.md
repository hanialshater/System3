# Luxury: absolute vs. aspirational

**evidence: sourced**

## Behavioral signal

BCG-Altagamma's True-Luxury Global Consumer Insights (11th edition, 2025) splits luxury buyers by spend: **"Top absolute"/"Total true-luxury"** buyers (€50k+/year on personal luxury) are under 1% of buyers but 23% of category value; **"aspirational"** consumers buy occasionally, spending under €5k/year.

Source: https://web-assets.bcg.com/51/92/68b6af8346629c05e586a734d857/bcg-altagamma-true-luxury-global-consumer-insights-report-2025-vpress-final.pdf

## Commentary implications

- These two sub-segments need different tone even within the same [../categories/premium-designer.md](../categories/premium-designer.md) category — "absolute" customers likely have deep brand history to reference (see [../patterns/for-loyal-customer-do-reference-history.md](../patterns/for-loyal-customer-do-reference-history.md)); "aspirational" customers are closer to a first-time, trust-building interaction (see [../patterns/for-unfamiliar-brand-do-surface-social-proof.md](../patterns/for-unfamiliar-brand-do-surface-social-proof.md)).
- Tone should be informational and restrained rather than persuasive — see [../commentary/explain-vs-persuade.md](../commentary/explain-vs-persuade.md). Overt enthusiasm reads as off-key at this tier.
- Maps loosely to Zalando's own "Cultured Elite" Z-Type — see [zalando-z-types.md](zalando-z-types.md).
