# Occasion, gift, and family shopper

**evidence: sourced**

## Behavioral signal

**Occasion/gift shoppers** are motivated by giving or by a specific event rather than steady self-use browsing — deadline-driven, narrow and fast session pattern, respond to gift guides/curated bundles.

Source: https://woveninsights.ai/site-blog/how-buyer-personas-change-based-on-seasonal-vs-occasion-based-shopping/

**Parent/family shoppers** in kids' apparel are pragmatic, balancing budget against comfort/durability; children outgrowing clothes every 6-9 months forces frequent replenishment (a *growth-cycle* purchase rhythm, not a trend-cycle one); 93% research online first; pain points include cross-brand sizing inconsistency and stock/listing mismatches.

Source: https://www.giliguise.com/blogs/journal/parent-shopping-behavior-kids-clothes

## Commentary implications

- For gift/occasion sessions: check the actual occasion match before assuming coverage — see [../patterns/for-occasion-need-do-check-collection-match.md](../patterns/for-occasion-need-do-check-collection-match.md). The need is often social/emotional as much as functional — see [../needs/jobs-to-be-done-functional-social-emotional.md](../needs/jobs-to-be-done-functional-social-emotional.md).
- For parent/family sessions: safe to reference concrete history directly ("last time you sized up to X") since the purchase rhythm is predictable and growth-driven — see [../patterns/for-loyal-customer-do-reference-history.md](../patterns/for-loyal-customer-do-reference-history.md). Cross-brand size inconsistency is a real, named pain point here — fit commentary is high-value, not optional.
