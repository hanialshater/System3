# Trend-seeker / fashion-forward

**evidence: sourced** (indirect — no single named industry study, evidenced via platform-level data; see below)

## Behavioral signal

High purchase frequency, low category affinity (buys opportunistically across categories rather than sticking to one), higher return rate. No major research house (McKinsey/BCG/Bain) publishes this exact archetype by name — it's best evidenced through ASOS's and H&M's customer bases: ASOS's ~25M active customers are ~75% Gen Z/younger-Millennial, and H&M's Gen Z/Millennial cohort drives ~45% of 2025 revenue, both described as fast, "buy-now, wear-now" shoppers who frequently refresh their wardrobe at accessible price points. It maps closely to Zalando's own "Street Snobs" Z-Type (see [zalando-z-types.md](zalando-z-types.md)).

Source: https://businessmodelanalyst.com/hm-target-market-analysis/

## Commentary implications

- Pairs naturally with the fast-moving aesthetic tags in [../styles/modern-aesthetic-tags.md](../styles/modern-aesthetic-tags.md), not the durable silhouette archetypes in [../styles/classic-style-archetypes.md](../styles/classic-style-archetypes.md).
- Discovery/exploration framing (see [../needs/discovery-vs-targeted-search.md](../needs/discovery-vs-targeted-search.md)) fits this persona better than targeted-search framing — they often don't know exactly what they want yet.
- Higher return-rate association means fit-claim caution still applies — high engagement doesn't mean high fit-confidence evidence. See [../commentary/hedging-fit-claims.md](../commentary/hedging-fit-claims.md).
