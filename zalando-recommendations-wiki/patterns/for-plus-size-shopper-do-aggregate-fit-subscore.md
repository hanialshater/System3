# For a plus-size / extended-size shopper, do show an aggregate fit subscore — not just a general rating

**evidence: sourced**

## Trigger

Extended-size filter applied, or browsing pattern matching [../personas/plus-size-inclusive.md](../personas/plus-size-inclusive.md).

## Do

Surface a fit-specific subscore distilled from reviews (runs small / true to size / runs large), ideally weighted toward reviewers in a similar size range, separate from the item's general star rating. Pair this with any occasion context present (see [for-occasion-need-do-check-collection-match.md](for-occasion-need-do-check-collection-match.md)) since this persona and occasion need co-occur often.

## Why

Baymard specifically recommends an aggregate "fit" subscore separate from the general quality rating, because a generic star rating doesn't answer the specific question a fit-anxious shopper has. `evidence: sourced` — https://baymard.com/blog/apparel-provide-aggregate-fit-subscore-in-reviews. This need is elevated for plus-size shoppers specifically: "fit anxiety" from inconsistent sizing across brands is a named, elevated driver in this segment, and offering extended sizing with good fit guidance lifts brand loyalty by ~31% and purchase frequency by ~24%. `evidence: sourced` — https://www.fastsimon.com/ecommerce-wiki/optimized-ecommerce-experience/supply-and-demand-challenges-in-the-womens-plus-size-market/

## Don't

- Don't reuse a fit subscore computed from the general (non-extended-size) reviewer population and present it as relevant to this shopper — the whole point is that sizing consistency differs by size range.
- Don't assume this persona skews older — the data says the opposite (55% of size 1X-3X buyers are under 35). Don't let tone or item selection default to an inaccurate demographic guess. See [../personas/plus-size-inclusive.md](../personas/plus-size-inclusive.md).
