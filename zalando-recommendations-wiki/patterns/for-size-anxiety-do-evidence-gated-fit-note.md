# For size anxiety, do an evidence-gated fit note — never a flat claim

**evidence: sourced** (the response mechanism, not just the underlying need)

## Trigger

Size chart opened more than once on the same item, especially combined with no add-to-bag, or an unfamiliar brand, or a plus-size/extended-size context (see [for-plus-size-shopper-do-aggregate-fit-subscore.md](for-plus-size-shopper-do-aggregate-fit-subscore.md)).

## Do

Surface a fit note whose confidence language is a direct function of real evidence: how much fit data exists for this item, how recent it is, whether it's measured on this exact article or extrapolated from other brands/products, and how consistent sizing has historically been for this brand. This is literally how production fit-confidence systems work (e.g. True Fit scores fit predictions on data volume, recency, extrapolation, and brand consistency). `evidence: sourced` — https://www.truefit.com/how-it-works

Use a small, fixed hedge vocabulary tied to the evidence tier — see [../commentary/hedging-fit-claims.md](../commentary/hedging-fit-claims.md) for the exact phrasing table.

## Why

Fit and sizing issues drive roughly 50-67% of fashion e-commerce returns, and "too small" alone accounts for about 34% of all returns — this is the single largest need in the category, not a minor one. `evidence: sourced` — https://koozee.ai/blog/fashion-ecommerce-return-rate. Resolving fit uncertainty *before* checkout measurably raises conversion and lowers returns; the problem is a data/confidence problem, not just a content problem. `evidence: sourced` — https://www.truefit.com/post/pdp-size-guidance-vs-fit-intelligence

## Don't

- Don't assert "runs true to size" without enough same-brand fit reports behind it. A false fit claim is worse than no claim — it costs more trust than a vague or absent one.
- Don't default to a generic size chart link when a targeted note is possible — Baymard's research found ~90% of apparel sites already fail to give users a way to judge size/fit properly, and a generic chart is exactly the kind of thing that already isn't working. `evidence: sourced` — https://baymard.com/blog/apparel-ecommerce-ux-research-launch
- Don't silently say nothing when evidence is thin. Say "we don't have enough fit data on this one yet" — an honest gap beats silence that reads as implicit confidence.
