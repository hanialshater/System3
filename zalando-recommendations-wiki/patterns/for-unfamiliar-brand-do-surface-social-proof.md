# For an unfamiliar brand, do surface third-party social proof — not brand copy

**evidence: sourced**

## Trigger

Customer is viewing an item from a brand they have no purchase or browse history with, especially combined with hesitation signals (long dwell time without add-to-bag, size chart opens, repeat visits without progress).

## Do

Lead with reviews, ratings, and aggregate buyer feedback rather than descriptive brand/marketing copy. If a fit claim is warranted, gate it per [../commentary/hedging-fit-claims.md](../commentary/hedging-fit-claims.md) — new-brand trust and fit-data depth often move together, since both depend on accumulated purchase history.

## Why

Consumers report 89% high/medium trust in word-of-mouth and 85% in ratings/reviews — 2-3x the trust placed in brand advertising or brand websites. When shoppers hit a negative review, nearly half keep researching until confident and over a third look for other reviewers to cross-check; a higher-rated, more-reviewed product beats a cheaper alternative in preference. `evidence: sourced` — https://www.bazaarvoice.com/blog/why-customer-testimonials-and-peer-reviews-are-key-to-shopper-trust-in-2025/. Because shoppers can't touch fabric or check construction online, they otherwise fall back on price/discount depth as a quality proxy — a worse substitute for the trust job than real social proof. `evidence: sourced` — https://fashionandtextiles.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40691-018-0128-2

## Don't

- Don't try to build trust with confident brand-voice copy ("beloved by thousands") that isn't itself traceable to a real review count or rating — see [../commentary/ground-claims-in-signals.md](../commentary/ground-claims-in-signals.md).
- Don't assume more product images solve a trust problem — the gap here is credibility, not visual information.
