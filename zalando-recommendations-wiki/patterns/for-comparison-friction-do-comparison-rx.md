# For comparison friction, do a direct comparison — not more items

**evidence: hypothesis** (the underlying need is `evidence: sourced`, see [../needs/comparison-friction.md](../needs/comparison-friction.md); the specific response mechanism proposed here has not yet been tested)

## Trigger

A customer repeatedly switches between a small, specific set of products (typically 2-3) across a session or across repeat visits. Time spent per page may be declining even as visit count rises — a sign they already know the content and are stuck deciding, not still exploring.

## Do

Show a direct comparison of the *specific items the customer is actually deciding between*, on the attributes their switching behavior suggests matter (if they've opened size charts on both, size/fit; if they've read far into descriptions, material/durability). Not a generic "similar items" carousel. If a confidence/trust gap remains after the comparison, a second, smaller insight note can follow — see [for-unfamiliar-brand-do-surface-social-proof.md](for-unfamiliar-brand-do-surface-social-proof.md) if one of the items is from an unfamiliar brand.

## Why

More items compounds the problem instead of solving it — a customer with comparison friction already has enough choice; what she's missing is a way to resolve the choice she has. Choice-overload research shows that above a certain assortment size, conversion drops even as more people browse: a 24-item display drew more visitors than a 6-item one but converted at 3% vs. 30%. `evidence: sourced` — https://www.crobox.com/blog/choice-overload — see [../needs/comparison-friction.md](../needs/comparison-friction.md) for the full research.

## Don't

- Don't rank a third, unrelated "similar item" above the comparison just because it scores well on engagement — see [../commentary/README.md](../commentary/README.md) on why click-optimizing an insight response is the wrong objective.
- Don't compare on generic attributes (price, brand, rating) if the customer's own behavior points at something more specific (fit, durability, color match to something they already own).
