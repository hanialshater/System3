# For a loyal or returning customer, do reference specific traceable history — not a vague taste profile

**evidence: sourced**

## Trigger

Repeat purchase history within a brand or category, an account with a real order history, or a customer matching [../personas/brand-loyalist.md](../personas/brand-loyalist.md).

## Do

Reference something concrete and specific the customer actually did — a kept item, a past purchase, a saved product — rather than an abstract style-profile summary. "Because you kept the slim chinos last time, here's…" beats "based on your style, we think…".

## Why

Stitch Fix and Trunk Club's styling notes are credible specifically because they're anchored to information the customer recognizes giving the system — saved notes, past feedback, kept-vs-returned items — not generic flattery. Trunk Club stylists explicitly track kept-vs-returned items and reference that history in later notes. `evidence: sourced` — https://www.stitchfix.com/women/blog/inside-stitchfix/stitch-fix-note-request/ · https://medium.com/unpacking-trunk-club/recommendation-systems-at-trunk-club-overview-part-1-cc5783e5ff4b. Making the data source legible ("based on the jackets you saved") rather than implying omniscient inference also avoids the privacy-concern discount that otherwise offsets personalization's trust benefit — roughly 82% of consumers report privacy concerns about how their data is used. `evidence: sourced` — https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/18/2/1073

## Don't

- Don't invent specificity — if the only real signal is "bought from this brand once six months ago," don't write copy that implies a deep, ongoing relationship.
- Don't over-personalize every touchpoint — see [../commentary/README.md](../commentary/README.md) on reserving detailed rationale for actual decision moments, not every card.
