# For an occasion need, do check the collection actually matches — don't assume coverage

**evidence: hypothesis** (the underlying gap is `evidence: sourced`; the check-before-assuming response is a proposal)

## Trigger

Browsing pattern tied to a specific event (wedding guest, job interview, vacation, holiday), often narrow and fast/deadline-driven compared to steady seasonal browsing.

## Do

Before routing the customer to a pre-built curated collection (e.g. "wedding guest dresses"), check whether it actually matches the specifics of their occasion — climate, formality level, need to travel-pack, dress code nuance. If it doesn't match, treat this as unmet demand (see [../GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md)) rather than serving a near-miss collection and calling it solved.

## Why

Retailers today mostly address occasion-driven need through editorial/curated landing collections rather than structured occasion-attribute filtering — treating occasion as a merchandising/content problem rather than a queryable product attribute. This leaves a real gap whenever the customer's specific occasion (e.g. "beach wedding, humid climate, need to travel-pack it") doesn't match any pre-curated collection. `evidence: sourced` — https://www.theknot.com/content/where-to-buy-wedding-guest-dresses. Occasion-based need is also frequently a *social and emotional* job, not just a functional one ("will I look right at this event," not just "do I own a garment") — see [../needs/jobs-to-be-done-functional-social-emotional.md](../needs/jobs-to-be-done-functional-social-emotional.md).

## Don't

- Don't treat "we have a collection with this name" as evidence the need is covered. Check the actual match.
- Don't answer occasion need with only functional-attribute commentary (fabric, cut) when the real question is social/emotional (will I look right, will I feel confident) — see [../needs/jobs-to-be-done-functional-social-emotional.md](../needs/jobs-to-be-done-functional-social-emotional.md).
