# Ground every claim in a real, nameable signal

**evidence: sourced**

## The rule

Every commentary sentence that implies "we know something about you" should be traceable, internally, to a specific signal — a saved item, a past return, a stated occasion, an applied filter. If you can't name the signal behind a claim, don't make the claim; drop to population-level framing instead (see [cold-start-copy.md](cold-start-copy.md)).

## Why

Stitch Fix and Trunk Club's styling notes are credible specifically because they reference something the customer recognizes giving the system, not generic flattery: Stitch Fix explicitly frames its notes as anchored to client-submitted "Fix Notes" and past feedback; Trunk Club stylists reference kept-vs-returned items directly ("because you kept the slim chinos last time…").

Sources: https://www.stitchfix.com/women/blog/inside-stitchfix/stitch-fix-note-request/ · https://medium.com/unpacking-trunk-club/recommendation-systems-at-trunk-club-overview-part-1-cc5783e5ff4b

Making the data source legible ("based on the jackets you saved") rather than implying omniscient inference also matters for a second reason: roughly 82% of consumers report privacy concerns about how their data is used, which can dampen or reverse the trust benefit of personalization if the source feels invisible or creepy rather than named.

Source: https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/18/2/1073

## Examples

- **Do:** "Based on the jackets you saved, here's one in a similar cut."
- **Don't:** "We know you'll love this."
