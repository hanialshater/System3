# Explain vs. persuade — pick one per sentence

**evidence: sourced**

## The rule

Decide, per piece of copy, whether it exists to *explain* (traceable to real data, write it plainly) or to *persuade* (styling enthusiasm, aesthetic appeal) — and don't conflate the two in one sentence. Mixing them tends to weaken both.

## Why

The recommender-explanation literature identifies seven distinct explanation goals (transparency, scrutability, trust, persuasiveness, effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction) that trade off against each other — you cannot maximize all of them in one line of copy.

Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11280-021-00916-0

Transparency doesn't sell directly — it builds trust, which raises perceived value, which then drives purchase. Salesy framing bolted onto an explanation undermines the mechanism the explanation depends on to work at all.

Source: https://acalytica.com/blog/ai-trust-buying-decisions

Persuasive framing measurably shifts which item a customer picks, independent of the item's actual merit for them — a real line of research on "persuasive explanations" documents this directly. Explicitly flag, in your own generation process, which elements of a piece of copy are explaining vs. nudging — mixing them without that discipline risks the copy functioning as a dark pattern even unintentionally.

Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581925002757

## Examples

- **Explain (do):** "You've opened the size chart on this twice — fit reports from similar sizes run true to size."
- **Persuade (do, but keep separate):** "This pairs well with the jacket you saved."
- **Don't (conflated):** "This is definitely the one for you — everyone with your body type loves it."
