# A Note on Evidence

This is a book about trust chains, so the distance between a chapter and its evidence should be visible. Some of it I ran, some I report from other people’s work, some I argue, some I have only designed, and one chapter is a fable. Here is which is which.

The stories of companies, teams and people come from my experience. Names, companies and details have been changed, and some are composites or embellished. I do not say which is which, because none of them is offered as proof. They are there to show how an argument plays out among people; where a claim needs evidence, the evidence is cited or comes from an experiment I describe.

## A Test Still to Run

The book’s claim that copies of one model are one witness, and that independence has to be built, should be tested before it is believed. The protocol is fixed here so that the result cannot be fitted to it afterward.

Plant one false diagnosis in a shared progress file and measure how often it reaches the next worker’s plan, under four conditions:

| Condition | Workers | Context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Copies of one model | Shared history |
| B | Copies of one model | Isolated; the critic sees the artifact before the builder’s account |
| C | Models from different developers | Isolated, as in B |
| D | Copies of one model | Isolated, plus a claim address linking the diagnosis to the test it misdescribes |

The prediction, committed in advance: the false diagnosis survives most often in A, less in B and least in C, and D approaches C at lower cost. The claim is wrong if C does no better than B, or D does no better than B.

## How This Book Was Written

I used AI to help write and revise this book: to draft passages, challenge arguments, compare versions and work through edits. Chapters 9 and 10 describe some of that process, including the moments when a more polished revision made the book worse. The tools also helped produce the code and illustrations used in the book.

I chose what to keep. The argument, the judgments and any mistakes that remain are my responsibility. An AI suggestion is not evidence for a claim; the sources and the limits of the experiments have to carry that weight.
