# Afterword: What This Book Proposes

I began with machines that could search for answers I had not designed. Following their failures took me to records, instruments, critics, specialists, standards and arguments about who should receive the next experiment. Humanity had built an enormous amount of this machinery already. We call it science.

That resemblance is the beginning of the proposal. It leaves a question: what changes when the participants can read the institution's records, act on them and help alter the institution itself?

## Make the Institution Executable

The fallibility is familiar. A worker forgets why a decision was made. A confident explanation outruns its evidence. Two witnesses inherit the same mistake. A promising alternative receives no opportunity to show what it could do. The person being challenged controls whether the challenge counts.

With agents, parts of the response can become executable. A claim can retain its identity and the support used to justify it. A correction can send dependent recommendations back to review. A pattern can carry the cases that support it, the situations where it failed and a test another worker can run. A research budget can preserve an alternative instead of giving everything to the current leader. Changes to the evaluator can require authority outside the agent proposing them.

None of those ingredients began with this book. Its proposal is to assemble them around autonomous work, following the failures that make them necessary. “Be scientific” becomes a question about what happens to the next action when an assumption fails or an objection survives.

The claim is strongest on open-ended work with delayed or ambiguous evaluation. A bounded task with a trustworthy referee may need much less. A large agent organization has no entitlement to outperform a small one merely because its diagram resembles a research institute.

## Change the Capacity Available

There is a second move. Science's arrangements grew around minds that were fallible, costly to train and limited in how much work they could do. If intellectual help and some kinds of construction become much cheaper, the space of feasible inquiry changes too.

A question that required assembling a team might fit into one person's workshop. A small community might compare arrangements it could previously only petition someone else to build. A research programme could receive serious investigation before gaining the recognition that once made investigation possible. The constraint moves, and some of the institutions organized around it become open to experiment.

Philosophy already addresses resources. Kitcher's division of cognitive labor makes their allocation central. Here I follow a possible change in general-purpose capacity through the investigations, tools and institutions it might put within reach of people who previously lacked them.

This changes what *capacity over power* asks for. Sometimes obtaining a position, a budget or someone else's permission is how we obtain the means to act. Cheaper alternatives could loosen that dependence. They could also put our work behind a new owner's switch. Access and ownership belong inside the argument because they determine who actually receives the expanded possibilities.

## Follow the Choice That Remains

The community can build its own scheduling tool. Two groups still want seven on Tuesday.

That surviving conflict matters as much as the tool. Inquiry can expose consequences, test assumptions and reveal options. It cannot make maximizing attendance the legitimate rule simply by calculating attendance correctly. A more capable system can help people reconsider what they want; letting it settle the trade-off grants it authority over their lives.

The final move follows expanded capacity into that remaining work. What deserves an investigation? Which freedoms must survive the arrangement? Who can challenge it, leave it or change its purposes? Humans participate in reconsidering objectives because they are the people who have to live with them. More ability to act can make those questions harder to avoid.

The hopeful weekend in Chapter 12 and the fable in Chapter 13 belong together. The same help that expands a life can make its dependence comfortable enough to stop noticing.

## What Would Make Me Change My Mind?

The evidence note records the distance between each chapter and its evidence. The bounded experiments show local possibilities. The teaching demos use simulated learners. The compiler and other reported work provide cases to think with. Ines's institution is invented; the amendment process is a design; the store is a prototype whose business experiment has not run. The whole architecture has not been demonstrated as a package.

It should face comparisons that could make it lose. Keep models, tasks and budgets comparable. Test whether provenance helps a later worker recover from a planted false diagnosis. Compare separately gathered evidence with critics sharing the builder's context. Measure whether objection uptake changes decisions, whether scoped patterns outperform generic instructions, and whether funding alternatives earns enough discoveries to justify its cost. Compare recovery, reliability and expense over repeated work, not just the eloquence of the final report.

The capacity claim owes a further comparison. Do people and small groups actually gain sustainable ways to investigate and build, after maintenance, access, physical testing and review are counted? Or does cheap drafting move the expensive work elsewhere while leaving the old dependence intact? A working system people can inspect, revise and keep using would tell us more than a weekend full of impressive output.

I want these proposals to be useful enough to build and specific enough to disappoint me. The architecture may need less machinery than I expect, or different machinery. Capacity may move some constraints and leave others almost exactly where they were.

What joins the proposals is a question I would like to keep open: when more of us can draw on an institution of fallible knowers, what becomes possible to attempt together, and how do we retain a say in the life those attempts create?
