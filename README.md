### Governing arc

As AI becomes more autonomous, control does not disappear. It moves upward—from actions, to search, to evidence, to institutions, to accumulated culture, to self-improvement, and finally to the evolving relationship with what humans want.

## The causal handoffs

The book should feel less like a collection of strong ideas and more like one problem becoming another:

**Stop specifying actions.** Can the agent search well?  
**Search works when judgment is cheap.** What happens when “better” has no clean score?  
**Build a problem-solving architecture.** Why should its components trust one another?  
**Add epistemic structure.** What happens when knowledge becomes social?  
**Build institutions.** How does a persistent institution avoid rediscovering everything every morning?  
**Externalize culture.** Once culture is executable, who is allowed to edit it?  
**Let the scaffold improve.** Who checks whether self-improvement remains aligned?  
**Automate alignment research.** What if the human signal itself is sparse, strategic, uncertain, and changing?  
**Treat intention as a moving relationship.** If that works, why should the human still operate the machinery?  
**Fluent autonomy.** The complexity remains; it becomes infrastructure.
