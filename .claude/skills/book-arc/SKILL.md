# How the author builds a book

*System 3* is the worked example. `references/system3-book-arc.md` maps it fully, with line
references. `resources/editorial/working-spine.md` and `resources/editorial/easter-egg-register.md`
are the author's own spine and seed register. Read them before proposing changes to this book.

## The architecture

1. **One thesis, withheld and then revealed.** The thesis of *System 3*: give AI autonomy and
   control moves up to the conditions, and the architecture that emerges from repairing
   autonomy's failures turns out to be science.

   The word *science* stays off the page for five chapters:
   - a riddle in the preface ("we have built it before… at least one loose cable");
   - clues that get warmer (Ch3 lists the ingredients without naming the institution);
   - a near-miss at the end of Ch5 ("Humanity had already spent centuries building a society of
     fallible knowers");
   - then a page of its own: **"We call it science."**

   The reader assembles the thesis before being told it. Late in the book, a chapter puts the
   thesis on trial (Ch10, "Where This Could Be Wrong").
2. **A second, human thread** runs underneath and becomes the main line after the reveal. As AI
   takes the work, human value moves from operator, to supervisor, to frontier-owner, to someone
   who wants things, to a person who needs no function at all ("My children do not need
   comparative advantage to justify dinner").
3. **Parts with one job each.** The part pages turn towards the next part and do not recap.

   | Part | Chapters | Job |
   |---|---|---|
   | Preface | | Frame, seeds and riddle |
   | I. Emergence | 1–3 | The machine owns the search |
   | II. Institutions | 4–5 | Trust for one knower, then for many |
   | Reveal | | "We call it science." |
   | III. Science Turns Inward | 6–8 | The institution remembers, amends itself, audits itself |
   | Interlude | | The threat changes from error to power (Lysenko); it raises the stakes without promising a fix |
   | IV. Human Purposes | 9–10 | What the institution cannot supply (desire), and how assistance keeps the human |
   | V. What the Capacity Is For | 11–12 | A worked product and a hoped-for future |
   | Alternative ending | 13 + coda | A fable as counterweight, then two lines |
4. **Epigraphs state each part's lesson before the part argues it.** Each one is a line lifted
   from that part's own chapters. Collected in book order, they make the Zen appendix, which
   reads as a compressed table of contents.
5. **A recurring map.** The five-layer stack is introduced in Ch3 and reprinted in Ch4 with
   System 3 across it. The book climbs it as a ladder and saves the top rung (desire) for
   Part IV.
6. **Seeds and payoffs.** Each thread recurs and then pays off:
   - coffee: preface, then Ch1, 2, 7, 9 and 10, then "Decaf." in the fable;
   - the octopus: Ch1, Ch4, Ch12, then the fable;
   - the camel: Ch4, then Ch11 and Ch12;
   - Reviewer 2: Ch1, then Ch2;
   - the loose cable: preface, Ch4, the real OPERA cable in Ch5, then the shark in the fable.

   The best seed resolves inside the thesis itself. **Never explain a payoff.**
7. **Hand-offs.** Every chapter ends on a question or image that the next chapter's opening
   walks through. The Ch3–Ch4 and Ch10–Ch11 hand-offs are examples.
8. **Scale and subject move from machine to institution to human.** Part I is the author at a
   desk. The middle is civilization (Mesopotamia, Qin, CERN). The end is a family dinner. The
   pivots are explicit: one knower to many (Ch4 to Ch5), the agents' clear objective to the
   human's unclear one (Ch8 to Ch9), the author's corrections to a customer who "files no
   correction. She leaves" (Ch10 to Ch11).
9. **Endings in two registers.** The fable restates the late themes as comedy and pays off most
   seeds, left unexplained. The coda pairs with the reveal ("We built scaffolds for ourselves
   for the same reason").

## Applying it to a new book

Work through these questions in order:

1. **What is the one thesis, and what word or reveal can be withheld?** Plant a riddle early,
   give clues that warm up, and make the reveal its own moment.
2. **What is the human thread,** and how does it shift as the argument advances? Write its
   stages as a ladder.
3. **Parts:** give each one a single job, in three to six parts. Write each part's epigraph,
   one line it will earn.
4. **A recurring map or model** the reader can climb.
5. **Seeds:** plan three to five, each planted early, recurring, then paid off, with at least one
   that resolves inside the thesis. Record them in a seed register.
6. **The chapter list:** a title and a one-line job for each chapter, and the hand-off from each
   chapter's last line to the next one's opening.
7. **An interlude** that changes the threat or the register, and an ending that is not a
   summary.
8. **Where the author's lived material carries each part.** Mark gaps as `[AUTHOR: …]`. Never
   invent his experiences.

## Weigh the parts

Count words per part. A part much shorter than its neighbours, or one that is asked to carry
both the human thread and a thesis trial, is a structural risk even when every chapter is good.
Also check that no story, case or invented company has two homes. When one does, make one
chapter the home and give the other a callback.

## Diagnosing an existing arc

Map the book onto the nine elements above, as a table with one row per element: present, weak
or missing, with file and line evidence. Then check that:
- the reveal comes after the clues, not before (search the chapters before the reveal for the withheld word itself);
- each part does one job;
- every seed pays off and no payoff is explained;
- every hand-off connects;
- the human thread doesn't disappear for more than one part;
- no chapter repeats another's story without adding a beat. Give each story one home and use
  callbacks elsewhere.

Report the three weakest points with evidence, and a fix for each. Keep fixes structural; leave
prose fixes to `development-edit`. Do not edit the manuscript unless asked.

## Output

For a new book, give in this order:
- the thesis;
- the withheld reveal;
- the human-thread ladder;
- the parts table (part, chapters, job, epigraph);
- the chapter list with hand-offs;
- the seed register (seed, planted, recurs, pays off);
- the interlude and ending;
- the `[AUTHOR: …]` needs.

For a diagnosis: the element table, the three weakest points with fixes, and anything protected
that must not change (such as Chapter 13 and the seeds).

Related skills: `chapter-arc` (inside a chapter), `development-edit` (the editing loop),
`author-voice` (prose).
