## Verdict

**The first section and the deploy incident are the strongest page-turning in the late book; the middle sags.** A reader cannot put the book down from "A card fills the screen" (ch11:7) to "Nobody who made that minute is a person" (ch11:23), or through the release that shipped while the founder was told it had not (ch11:89–93). The ending returns to the minute and lands.

A reader will put it down in three places: the store prelude, which promises a story it never tells and then becomes the source of the chapter's later callbacks (ch11:43–53); the paragraph of fourteen numbers (ch11:57–61); and the refrain of "not run", which by the second half lets the reader predict how each paragraph ends (ch11:83–145). Smaller stalls come from caveats placed as the last sentence of a paragraph whose point came earlier (ch11:81, 91, 127).

### The three findings that matter most

1. **(M8, must)** The store passage (ch11:43–53) is a put-down point, and three later payoffs rest on it: "The best idea in the store was about the objective" (117), "Both ideas began in the store" (119), "When I designed the store, the first test I wanted…" (107). The reader was never shown the store.
2. **(M14, should)** The "not run / not measured / no result" refrain occurs at least seven times. By "Willing to Lose" the reader expects it, and the last absence, the one the ending needs, no longer feels new.
3. **(M9, should)** "A Company in a Week" opens its company story with fourteen numbers in one paragraph (ch11:57) before its best hook, "testimony from a worker who no longer exists" (ch11:59).

## Findings

### M1. The opening cannot be put down. Severity: could (keep)

- **Location:** ch11:5–17.
- **Text:** "Nothing else on the card works until you press one." · "So you guess. Everyone guesses something, even the people who are sure they would never fall for it." · "If you picked 99%, you are in good company, and some of it has medical degrees."
- **Why it works:** the reader is made to commit before being told anything, and is then shown the answer through their own actions in "the town". This is the chapter's engine. The empty-town detail ("You have a positive result in a town with no sick people") is the moment the reader leans in.
- **Suggestion:** protect it through later passes.

### M2. The reveal paragraph adds a second number at the moment the first lands. Severity: should

- **Location:** ch11:15.
- **Text:** "A positive means about 9%." … "At 1 in 100, ten of twenty positives are sick, about half. At 1 in 10 it is 99 of 108. At the bottom, in grey, the card adds that in a 1978 study at Harvard Medical School the most common answer to a similar question was 95%, when the right answer, if the test catches every case, was about 2%."
- **Why it stalls:** the reader has just earned "about 9%". Within three sentences come two more towns and then a different right answer, "about 2%", with a condition attached. Many readers will stop to reconcile 9% and 2% and lose the pleasure of the reveal. Line 17 then has to recover it.
- **Suggestion:** let the 9% and the one-sentence moral stand alone, and give the Harvard aside its own beat, after or as line 17's set-up. Writer's call how.

### M3. The recall question is an open loop the chapter never closes. Severity: should

- **Location:** ch11:19, echoed at ch11:153.
- **Text:** "Of the people who test positive, roughly how many have it? Almost all, about half, or about 1 in 100?"
- **Why it matters:** asking is good pull. Not answering is a choice the reader will notice: someone who flips back at the end of the chapter finds nothing. If the book answers it later, the reader needs a hint that it will; if not, the chapter should close it.
- **Suggestion:** author to decide (see easter-eggs record F8, which asks whether this is a deliberate seed). Either signal the deferral or close the loop by the last section.

### M4. The first section ends on a turn that carries the reader over. Severity: could (keep)

- **Location:** ch11:21–23.
- **Text:** "Its own file budgets eighty seconds, which is the first claim in it I would check." · "Nobody who made that minute is a person."
- **Suggestion:** keep. The best section end in the chapter.

### M5. The claims inventory is the first place a reader skims. Severity: could

- **Location:** ch11:27.
- **Text:** "The other eight cover what “99% accurate” means here, the expected counts, the half and the ninety-two, the rounding on the stage, the Harvard study, the sentence the reveal ends on, and the question that comes back days later."
- **Why it stalls:** an eight-item list of things the reader cannot see, after an arithmetic expression set in prose ("0.99 × 0.001 divided by the same thing plus 0.01 × 0.999"). Line 29, the "Why trust this?" sheet, does the same work through something the reader can picture.
- **Suggestion:** shorten the inventory; let the sheet carry it.

### M6. The gate caveat arrives as procedure. Severity: could

- **Location:** ch11:37.
- **Text:** "The file has the same role name on both sides and cannot say whether the second look came from a fresh instance or from the same one on a different day."
- **Why it stalls:** ch11:35 ("one witness wearing different coats") is vivid; 37 restates the same limit at the level of fields and dates. The point is strong and the reader needs it; the paragraph makes them work for it.
- **Suggestion:** lead with the consequence, compress the mechanics.

### M7. The chapter boundaries no longer hand over. Severity: should

- **Location:** ch10 last line → ch11:1–7; ch11:153 → ch12:79.
- **Text:** ch10 ends "I spent years building systems that decide what to show her." The old Chapter 11 picked that up in its first sentence ("After years working on ranking and recommendations, I built a prototype store…"). The new chapter opens on "Chapter 11: Guess First" and a medical-test card, and picks the thread up only at ch11:43. In Chapter 12, line 79 still says "Chapter 11’s store that builds itself still needs its business experiment".
- **Why it matters:** the swerve at the opening can work as surprise, because the card is strong. But the ch10 cue is left hanging for forty lines, and a reader arriving in Chapter 12 is pointed back to a chapter they did not read.
- **Suggestion:** decide whether the swerve is intended; if so, let the store's late entrance (43) acknowledge the ch10 cue. Ch12:79 needs to match whatever Chapter 11 becomes (routing: head of book; see easter-eggs record F2 before touching ch12:77–79). The ending's "she" (ch11:153) does pick up ch10's "her"; that thread is worth keeping.

### M8. The store passage: a put-down point that later sections lean on. Severity: must

- **Location:** ch11:43–53, with payoffs at 107, 117 and 119.
- **Text:** "After years working on ranking and recommendations, I built a prototype store because the idea would not stay on the page." · "My complaint was simpler. The page is smart inside the modules and surprisingly dumb between them." · "But once you can imagine a store learning that way, it is hard not to imagine the same thing in research, education or the organization doing the building." · "System 3 Company is the last two at once…"
- **Why a reader stops here:** the chapter has just built momentum on the company. It jumps back in time to a prototype the reader never sees (no customer, no screen, no moment), states a complaint whose example was cut ("dumb between them" lands abstractly without one), and hands over through a list ("the last two at once") rather than an event. Then the chapter keeps citing the store as if the reader had been there: "When I designed the store, the first test I wanted was deliberately boring" (107), "The best idea in the store was about the objective" (117), "Both ideas began in the store" (119), and the mission "quotes one of my store sentences back at me" (119). Each is a payoff without a setup.
- **Suggestion:** one of two shapes, author's choice: give the store one concrete moment before the company (the AUTHOR comment at 45 and Ask 2 at 51 ask for exactly this; it has to be the author's, not invented), or reduce the later store references to what three paragraphs can carry. As drafted it does neither.

### M9. Fourteen numbers before the hook. Severity: should

- **Location:** ch11:57–61.
- **Text:** "Its first commit landed at 22:51 UTC on 12 October 2026. By 14:21 on 17 October, four days and fifteen and a half hours later, the main branch held 2,718 commits. The founding decision created 26 roles, 25 agents and the founder. By the night of the 13th there were 72. As of 17 October the org data lists 99. …" then 1,283 lessons, 14 subjects, 720 published, 269 notes, 113.1 million tokens.
- **Why a reader stops:** the reader is asked to hold numbers with nothing to do with them. The one comparison that surprises ("eight subjects and about twenty lessons" planned against "1,283 lessons in 14 subjects") is buried mid-paragraph. The best line of the section, "testimony from a worker who no longer exists: check what you rely on." (59), comes after.
- **Suggestion:** keep the planned-against-actual contrast and the worker line up front; move the rest down or out. "The records hold no figure for money." (61) is a good section-ending sting; keep it.

### M11. The hub story ends on its provenance, not its punch. Severity: could

- **Location:** ch11:81.
- **Text:** the quote "The org chart says 20 lanes; in practice there was one dispatcher who asked managers questions." is followed by "Those are its counts at the time, and it labels its own table testimony-grade. ### M12. The deploy incident: the chapter's best story, deflated at its peak. Severity: should

- **Location:** ch11:89–93.
- **Text:** "To explain why the release was being held out of the log, the message mentioned the marker commits, and to mention them it quoted the marker." · "For about twenty minutes production ran a release its owner had not approved, while he was being told nothing had been deployed. The content matched what he approved minutes later. That part was luck." Then: "The hosting service’s own commit statuses show all of this, and a script in the press’s evidence folder reruns the old filter against the saved messages and reaches the same verdict, so this incident is run as well as recorded."
- **Why:** this is where the reader cannot stop; the conscientious message that triggers the deploy is irony a reader will retell. "That part was luck." is the beat, and the evidence sentence that follows lets the air out. Line 93's coda ("nothing listens for the word any more") is excellent and should be the section's last impression, which it is.
- **Suggestion:** let "That part was luck." end its paragraph; place the evidence sentence where it does not interrupt (end of section, or the note on evidence).

### M13. "Willing to Lose" sets up a contest and immediately cancels it. Severity: could

- **Location:** ch11:103–107.
- **Text:** "The arrangement the company has to beat is the dullest one available." then "As of 17 October nobody has run it." and "The company has eight numbered experiments, and none of them has a result." Then "The company went to fourteen subjects in its first week."
- **Reading:** the set-up is strong and the sting at 107 works. The cancellation is the honest result; it only stalls because of M14.

### M14. The refrain of absence. Severity: should

- **Locations and text:** ch11:83 "Nobody has measured whether it works." · 93 "As of 17 October all four are still in the backlog." · 105 "As of 17 October nobody has run it." and "none of them has a result." · 113 "It is designed and has not been run." · 117 "for now the fourth line of the company’s objective measures nobody." · 121 "As of 17 October it is drafted and not ratified." · 145 "It was designed on the day the company started. It has not been run."
- **Why a reader stops:** each is true and each matters, but by the fourth the reader can predict how a paragraph will end, and anticipation is what keeps pages turning. Worse, the ending (145–153) depends on the last absence, the five-learner test, feeling like a discovery, and by then it is the seventh.
- **Suggestion:** keep the absences that are stakes: the single-agent baseline (105) and the human learners (141–145). Compress or fold the others into a single sentence that counts them once.

### M15. The bug-planting paragraph is the longest stall in the chapter. Severity: should

- **Location:** ch11:113.
- **Text:** "its builder planted 10 bugs in its own engine and an operations reviewer planted 12 different ones … So the press wrote a new harness for the 15 of those 22 bugs it could reproduce exactly, ran it against the company’s committed tests at its latest commit, and all 15 were caught, each with exactly the failing count the record printed." It ends on three caveats in a row.
- **Why:** a reader who is not an engineer has to track three counts and three actors to reach a result the paragraph then limits. The one sentence with pull is "That shows the tests can fail."
- **Suggestion:** shorten to the result, that sentence, and the single limit that matters (the first experiment, designed and not run).
