## Chapter 7: what changed

The original chapter already had an intelligible history: responsibilities once performed by the teacher move into the learning machinery. The revision preserves the examples and research traditions instead of replacing them with a short taxonomy.

After Good's argument, a prompt-editing example distinguishes changing the system, improving it under an evaluation, and improving its ability to find further improvements. Motivation moves forward. Competition's pressure on research practices now sits with the Red Queen discussion, where its mechanism is visible.

The original “Shadow History” repeated failures already explained beside curiosity, continual learning, simulation and learned judges. That inventory goes. Its consequential new idea survives at the gradebook: a mistaken evaluator can select a process that amplifies the mistake. The coffee experiment's frozen evaluator then meets the harder case of an evaluator that deserves amendment.

The constitutional section now carries the ethical limits and changing interests that previously appeared in a separate late discussion. The ending asks how much of the growing research institution a human can judge, preparing Chapter 8's actual experiment.

These remain bounded experimental results.

- [Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717): checked thirteen models and intervention in residual-stream activations. The new mechanism explanation does not imply retraining was required.

This was a focused source check, not a new independent audit of every citation. The Gödel Machine passage was narrowed so that experimental search is an empirical approach to the question, not a proof of the same guarantee. No personal episode or experimental outcome was added to Chapter 10.

## Overall judgment

Use the revised chapters as the base. Chapter 7 gains most from the consolidation of its ending; Chapter 8 gains most from giving the reader a live evidential problem before the survey; Chapter 10 gains most from putting aspiration in contact with the actual record.

The sequence now carries a more definite progression: a system can revise its own machinery; its growing research activity needs oversight; the human judgment involved in that oversight can itself change; the interface must therefore manage both delegation and continued human involvement. Chapter 11 then asks how that proposal survives a constrained product setting.

The best estimate from this pass is **Chapter 7: about 9; Chapter 8: about 8.5; Chapter 10: about 8.5**. The remaining uncertainty is principally reader experience through Chapter 8's technical middle, not missing safeguards or a need for another generic warning paragraph. These edits justify choosing the new versions. They do not establish a new overall score for System 3 or settle its ranking against another published book.
