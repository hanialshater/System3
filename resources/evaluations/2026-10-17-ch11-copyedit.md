## Queries

**For the author (or the head of book, as a house-style decision)**

- **Q1. "%" versus "percent."** The style sheet says to use "percent" in prose and "%" only in tables. Chapters 9, 10 and 12 print no "%" in prose, but this chapter prints it 21 times. Many of these are the card's own strings (the buttons "about 99%" … "about 9%", "The test is 99% accurate", "‘99% accurate’ describes the test"), and those should stay as the card shows them. The rest are narrative: line 9 ("flags 99% of sick people… 1% of healthy people"), 15 ("1% of the 999", "about 9%", "95%"), 17 ("picked 99%"), 31 ("a 9% chance"), 39 ("the 9%"), 91 ("about 99%"), 97 ("8%", "35%"). Converting only the narrative ones would put "99%" and "99 percent" in neighbouring sentences of a chapter about that number. So I have **not** applied the rule. Do you want (a) "percent" in narrative and "%" in card strings, (b) "%" throughout this chapter as a deliberate exception, recorded in the style sheet, or (c) something else?
- **Q2. Numeral style.** The style sheet wants words for one to one hundred in narrative prose. This chapter mixes the two forms: "14 subjects" (line 67) against "fourteen subjects" (line 119) and Ask 9; "26 roles: 25 agents" and "99 roles" (67); "48 hours" (91); "made 10" (97); "10 bugs… 12 different ones… 15 of the 22" (127); "ten of twenty… 99 of 108" in one clause (15). Some of these are defensible as numerals (statistics sentences next to 1,283 and 720; comparison with 852; problem statements). The brief for this pass said not to touch numbers, and a fact-checker is matching wording in parallel, so I changed none. Please decide whether to bring them to the style sheet after the fact-check closes. The "14"/"fourteen" pair is the one a reader is most likely to notice.
- **Q3. Line 111, antecedent of "it".** "It shows that nothing unchecked was marked published. Lessons still in review are on the site as well, tagged “In review,” by the founder’s choice. And it cannot show that “checked” means more than…" The "it" in the last sentence refers to the gate result two sentences back, but the sentence in between is about the site, so a reader may hesitate. A possible fix, if you want one, is "And the count cannot show…". This is a meaning query, so I have not changed it.
- **Q4. Line 147, "if he was wrong."** Formal usage would take the subjunctive ("if he were wrong"). I left it as written because the indicative reads as your conversational register. Change it only if you prefer the formal form.

**For the fact-checker (flagged, not checked)**
