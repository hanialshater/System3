# Chapter to post

A post is not a chapter summary. It carries one idea a reader can retell, in Hani's voice, and leaves the book something to give.

## How he likes to work

- **Discuss before drafting.** Ask for the topic, the sacred object, the outside lens and whether there's a practical turn, then agree the beats. Don't hand over an unsolicited full draft.
- **Build beat by beat**, checking each beat's job ("this beat should make the reader nod along, so the turn hurts").
- **He makes the final word choices.** Offer options with the trade-off; recommend one.
- **De-LLM is the main editing instruction**: balanced parallels, neat antithesis, over-explained transitions, rule-of-three lists, every paragraph ending on the same punchy fragment, machine-even rhythm. Use the de-llm skill.
- When he pastes his own edited version back, edit that version, not yours.

## The Coffee & Commute shape

1. **A borrowed lens from outside engineering** (Zahavi's handicap principle, Frankfurt on bullshit, Žižek and Lacan's symptom and sinthome). Don't repeat a lens he has used.
2. **Pointed at a sacred object** in tech culture that people nod along to without thinking (SOLID, signalling, metrics).
3. **Grant it first.** Make the object attractive enough that the reader buys in ("SOLID is good. Every letter points at a real wound somebody bled from."); too little praise makes the fall cheap, too much makes it a bait-and-switch.
4. **The concrete break.** A small real example the reader watches go wrong (the widget controller split into controller, creator and router, and the code gets worse). Ground it in the real source; fetch it.
5. **Why it persists after people know it's hollow.** This is the move that makes the posts land ("high-functioning bullshit survives because the conviction is authentic"; "SOLID survives its first death as a ghost").
6. **Turn the knife inward.** Self-implication keeps the post from becoming the tribal flag-planting it criticises ("stay skeptical of narratives, including this one").
7. Optional practical turn (the peacock piece had one).

Borrowed vocabulary (the Real, symptom, sinthome, second death) must be load-bearing structure, introduced differently each time so it doesn't read as one template stamped five times.

## Voice

Short lines and white space where they earn it; stacked rhetorical questions; casual asides; loose grammar kept; a recurring self-description used as a frame (the zombie "for the greater good") closed in the last line; "a guy on a train, not a philosopher on a mountain". Header `☕️ Coffee & Commute — Title`, then the date.

## From a chapter or essay

- **One idea.** Pick the most retellable one (for the System 3 article: the tongue–ear test, the Saussure punchline, the System 1/2/3 framing).
- **Length.** About 800 words for a standalone LinkedIn post linking to the full piece. Long posts: offer the two cheapest cuts.
- **Threads.** One beat per post: hook; the human-scale example (the Alberto chain); what it looks like in code; the link. Write for the audience he has: engineers who get excited about a `MetaBeliefs` dictionary. Lean into it.
- **Don't spend the book's twists.** The reveal (*We call it science*), seeded payoffs and Chapter 13's fable stay in the book unless he chooses to use them. A trailer shows the explosion, not the plot twist.
- **Overlap.** Note when a post reuses a line from the book or the Arabic blog. Decide which piece owns a joke (he has accepted the Leibniz / uncle-joke overlap).
- **Exposure.** Posts are public immediately. No employer-identifying product detail; check with the risk-audit rules.
- **Arabic version.** Use levantine-translate; his Arabic register is a different, often stronger writer, so adapt rather than translate line by line.

## Delivering

Put a finished post in a file only when he asks for something he'll publish from; otherwise keep it in chat. Flag any liberty you took with tone about a real person (the affectionate "flamethrower" line about Copeland) so he can pull it.
