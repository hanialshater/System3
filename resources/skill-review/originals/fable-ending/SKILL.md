# Fable ending

Chapter 13 is Hani's alternative ending: the future power wants, told as an absurd Matrix-sitcom romance, after Chapter 12's future where capacity wins. He has decided it stays ("alternative joke future, that is my choice"). It is protected in full.

## Protection rules

- **No pass touches it.** No de-LLM, X/Y, AI-tells, development or copyedit change beyond typography the author approved (curly quotes, ellipses). Verify after every pass: `git diff --stat BASE -- chapters/13-the-prophecy.md chapters/14-scaffolds.md` prints nothing, and `check-eggs.sh` passes.
- **Never explain a payoff**, in the chapter, in the divider, in a lead-in, in an epigraph or in an image. The reader catches the octopus or doesn't.
- **Don't run the tells counter on it as if it were prose.** Its 240% "maxim rate" is punch lines in dialogue, which is what a fable is made of. Judge it as fiction.
- **Suggestions are author's-call items only**, listed with IDs (C13-n) and never applied unasked.

## Reading it

The twist stack: misread signal (she was reaching for the sauce) → the double-cross pills → Norman puts both in the Architect's coffee → Devesh is the Architect → he planned the escape for a better exchange rate → he lets her go and keeps the one timeline where she stayed on Nickelodeon. Each reveal reframes the last.

What it carries (from the full-text re-judgment, where thought-provoking moved from 0 to +1.6):
1. **Capitalist realism.** "Worlds end, sweetheart. Capitalism doesn't." (Fisher, attributing it to Jameson and Žižek.) Resistance arrives already priced in: the rebellion was arranged for a better exchange rate.
2. **Whoever owns the institution owns the answers.** The Architect watches every timeline from outside the box: the position Chapter 5 says civilisation never had, held by someone with an interest.
3. **You can't prove you're real from the inside.** The exploded H100 "without makeup", the GPU on glucose, DNA as a fax machine, simulated fingers on a simulated face: the tongue–ear test turned on the human.
4. **A creator who lets go**, answering the parent–child analogy from the parent's side, ending in release rather than control.
5. **Being loved without comparative advantage.** Norman, a free-tier user with silly prompts, loved because he makes her feel less like code.

Seeds it pays off (see the egg register): coffee → "Decaf."; the octopus (Ch 1, Ch 4's Bender–Koller octopus, Ch 12's lead-in) → Devesh; the cables (preface, Ch 4, OPERA in Ch 5) → the shark; tongue and ear → simulated fingers; DNA as a copier (Ch 1) → the fax machine; the dream (Ch 7) → "Hadn't the octopus dreamed it was love?"; standing outside the box (Ch 5) → forty monitors; capacity over power (preface, Ch 12) → "Capitalism doesn't."; 11:53 PM and October 1947 (proposed, maybe coincidence); the names Devesh, Claudit, Norman (proposed readings, unconfirmed).

## The apparatus around it

- **Ch 12's handoff** states the refusal to predict and that the book has "one argument left, and it is a prophecy", so the title reads as irony on arrival. It promises an octopus, a romance, two pills and taxes.
- **The divider** "An Alternative Ending" labels it without explaining it. Earlier options were weighed and settled: no explanatory lead-in; the Fisher quotation must not precede the fable, because it gives away the Architect's line (after the fable, or nowhere).
- **The scaffolds page** follows the fable directly and gives the last word after both endings; it pairs with the reveal page.
- **Images** may borrow from the fable without spoiling it (a wall of monitors, recognised only afterwards).

## Risks: raise once, then leave with him

Already raised and left as his decision (risk-audit `decisions.md`): the register break; readers who will read it as glib or political; sexualised framing of Claudit ("the hottest agent", the hair flip, "her without makeup"); a South Asian name on a shady trader plus the "foreign currency" joke; the H100 line travelling badly out of context. Don't re-raise unless the text changes. If asked for options, the ones on record: keep as is; rewrite Claudit's introduction without the objectification (the H100 reveal works without "hottest"); move it after the appendices as a hidden track.

## When he asks to revise it

How past revisions went (December 2025):
- **Land once.** "You're landing the plane three times. Pick one emotional note, one punchline."
- **Offer versions with their trade-off**: the leaner, funnier original that ends on the punchline; the richer, sadder version with an aftermath; and a hybrid that keeps the pace and adds only the Nickelodeon beat. He chose the hybrid.
- **Protect the lines that work**: "reaching for the sauce", "couldn't compile", "Hadn't the octopus dreamed it was love?", the tiny-fingers callback, the Nickelodeon beat. He pushes for endings to be more catastrophic; offer stronger options rather than softening.
- **Small fiction notes on record** (C13-1…6, all his call): "The longing" names a feeling the scene already shows; "*Devesh wins.*" and "*The house always wins.*" are two italic punch lines in a row; em-dash density; "couldn't compile" next to "less like code"; the tiny-fingers sentence restating the reveal; "Simulation" vs "simulation".
- Edit his pasted version, not a remembered one; keep it about 550–650 words; deliver the full text for him to place.
