## 1. Recap openings

A chapter that retells the previous chapter's ending before starting. Chapter 4's first eight paragraphs retold the last page of Chapter 3, down to "Nothing crashes." The chapter really started at the camel.

> ~~Deep Mode left us with a system in which almost everything could work and the whole thing could still be wrong. … Nothing crashes. The first claim was false. … At some point, it has to trust. … So before we design another architecture, consider a camel.~~
> → Before we design another architecture, consider a camel.

## 2. Signposts

Sentences that announce what the next sentence will do. The heading or the next sentence already does it.

- ~~There is a question that exposes something important about the difference between us and a language model:~~
- ~~This changes how we should think about skills, tools and memory.~~
- ~~There is an obvious problem with all this.~~ (the heading announced it)
- ~~Those questions will occupy much of this book. For now, the important point is simpler.~~
- ~~That is the magic of the form, and it is worth looking at closely.~~ → That is the magic of the form.
- ~~This matters far beyond circle packing.~~ A system can become expert…
- ~~At its most compressed,~~ the danger is…
- ~~Wittgenstein helps draw the other side of the picture. His later philosophy~~ → Wittgenstein's later philosophy pulled…
- ~~Look back at the marks. They thin out as the patterns climb…~~ (tells the reader what to notice; Claude's own addition)

Measured effect, Chapter 4: 2.05 → 0.87 signposts per 1,000 words. The author's cleanest chapter ran at 0.00.

## 3. Re-explaining and labelled recaps

A point lands, then is said again in other words, or a story is followed by a summary of what it showed.

- Planck's quote says opponents die out. Next line: ~~In Planck's account, opponents need not be persuaded. They can be outlived.~~
- Bing: "The count was right. The cheerful interpretation was wrong." Then: ~~We had given the interpretation its own address. Now we know why it needed one.~~ The paragraph should end on the fact; the reader makes the connection.
- After the octopus: ~~I do not want to turn Saussure and Wittgenstein into action figures… But they give us two useful lines. Saussure's line: … Wittgenstein's line: …~~
- End of Chapter 4: ~~Some claims sit close to direct interaction. Others arrive through testimony. … Flatten all of that into equally confident language and something important disappears.~~ (lists every kind of claim the camel section had just walked through)
- Navier–Stokes: "the other problems lost workers, not been refuted" was made three times in three paragraphs. Keep the first.
- A *Therefore* followed by three sentences explaining it: cut the three.

## 4. Paragraph-closing morals and doubled verdicts

The loudest tell. In Chapter 6, 91 of 103 paragraphs of three or more sentences ended on a short general line. When every paragraph lands on a quotable line, none of them land, and the good ones get lost among the dutiful ones.

Dutiful (cut):
- ~~The questions left behind remained unanswered.~~
- ~~It shows that the organization of participation can change which work enters a field.~~
- ~~Faster discovery depends partly on what the last investigation leaves usable.~~
- ~~A criticism had travelled further than the institution's willingness to act on it.~~
- ~~Reliability is conditional.~~ ~~Now the belief is challengeable.~~ ~~The model inherits the residue.~~

Doubled verdicts:
- Frequency is not verification. ~~Statistical dominance is not epistemic authority.~~
- ~~The model is a mirror; the architecture determines what it reflects.~~ (stacked right before the bold "The model stays hollow")
- ~~The important shift is not speed. It is who owns the next idea.~~ (the sentence before already said it)

Good closers that survived because they are jokes or new information: "preserve the wrong lesson at industrial speed", "a decorative conscience", "we have successfully parallelized the experience of being ignored", "It is branding", "Otherwise I am decorative governance", "More what?"

## 5. Reflexive hedging

Pre-emptive disclaimers, each defensible alone, together reading as a writer bracing for objections instead of making a claim. Chapter 6 had 49 "does not / cannot / is not" sentences, about one every two to three paragraphs; the pass took it to 39.

Cut:
- ~~The reviewers can still be wrong.~~
- ~~This does not establish that the departed scientists had been wrong.~~
- ~~They do not guarantee that it is wise.~~
- ~~A new vocabulary does not entitle its author to victory.~~
- ~~We should not treat mortality as a scientific method, but~~ we should ask…
- ~~I do not know what was said in the room at Bing, so take what follows as the general case.~~ A result like Bing's…
- An LLM-written explanation produced after the fact ~~may be helpful, but it~~ cannot substitute…

Kept, because it carries weight: "A prediction does not become an experimental observation by being stored beside a billion others."

## 6. Anaphoric runs, question runs, tricolons

Fold, don't delete; the content is usually the author's.

> ~~We test things. We build instruments. We execute code. We compare claims with records. We ask other people. We preserve failures. We create procedures that make some errors harder to hide…~~
> → We test things, build instruments, ask other people and preserve our failures.

> ~~Sometimes I needed another attempt. Sometimes I needed information. Sometimes the search had become too narrow. Sometimes the representation itself… Sometimes the objective needed to change. Sometimes I needed to see the artifact from another mind.~~
> → Sometimes I needed another attempt, sometimes information. Sometimes the search had become too narrow, the representation was constraining what we could imagine, or the objective needed to change. Sometimes I needed to see the artifact from another mind.

> ~~Perhaps I hate this week. Perhaps I hate my manager. Perhaps I hate the profession. Perhaps I want more freedom. Perhaps I want status. Perhaps I am exhausted.~~
> → Perhaps I hate this week, or my manager, or the profession. Perhaps I want freedom, or status. Perhaps I am exhausted.

> You decide what problem she owns, provide context, set constraints and agree on what success looks like. (four "You…" sentences folded; both jokes kept)

Question runs: four questions → the strongest two ("Where did it work? Where did it fail?"). Tricolons: cut the weakest item (~~A mathematician follows an analogy because the structure looks interesting.~~ A scientist…).

Not a tell: Chapter 5's eight-item "It remembered. It was…" run is purposeful enumeration that builds to the count.

## 7. X/Y antitheses

Hani diagnosed this family himself ("the X the Y pattern"). It needs its own pass. Four shapes:

**Paired parallel sentences**
- ~~The names arrived. The catalogue arrived.~~ → The names and the catalogue arrived.
- ~~Every worker is temporary. The institution can be remarkably permanent.~~ (cut; the next paragraph shows it)
- ~~The count was right. The cheerful interpretation was wrong. The task-completion test we proposed could have exposed the problem; another audit of the count would not.~~ → Only the task-completion test we proposed could have caught it.

**X, not Y / not only**
- Three events~~, not one~~: a decision…
- The other problems had ~~lost workers, not been refuted~~ → only lost workers.
- alternative representations~~, not only~~ → as well as alternative answers.
- It belongs to a community's criticism~~, not to any single mind,~~ and it has…
- **Therefore: change what the next worker inherits~~, not only the worker~~.**

**Semicolon antitheses**
- ~~Bad storage forgets by deletion; bad retrieval forgets by attention.~~ (cut; the example carries it)
- ~~The scoreboard survived; the education of the person standing in front of it changed.~~ → What changed was the education of the person standing in front of it.
- ~~science does not set its own priorities; whoever pays for it does.~~ → whoever pays for science sets its priorities.
- a specific fatal flaw is enough to reject the proof~~; favorable verdicts from other reviewers cannot cancel it~~ → , however many other reviewers approve it.

**"X is not Y. It is Z."**
- ~~Human knowledge is not simply a pile of facts. It is~~ → Human knowledge is **epistemologically stratified**.
- ~~The example is silly. The difference is not.~~
- ~~The missing ingredient is not punishment for models. It is architecture that…~~

Kept on purpose: "Nobody is lying. The hypothesis has been fitted to the result." "The objection can now survive its author. So can the assumption it challenges." "Composition Is Not Ranking With a New Hat."

## 8. Verb monocultures ("when X becomes Y")

Chapter 12 used "become/became" 36 times, once every 140 words: the chapter's default verb for change. A pass took it to 4, each doing a job ("the workaround became the process", "become furious… about a sandwich", "local control can become local domination", the closing line). Everywhere else, state the specific thing that happened: "anyone could download a model", "needs went into a feature-request queue", "explanations wear out". Heading "When Difficulty Becomes Infrastructure" → "Built Around Difficulty".

Check other verbs the same way: "turns into", "emerges", "shifts", "matters".

## 9. Opener tics

Chapter 6 had seven paragraphs opening with "Suppose". Keep two or three; recast the rest:
- ~~Suppose the agent recorded the result… Then an automatic correction has no link~~ → If the agent recorded the result and left out the measurement assumption, an automatic correction has no link to follow.
- ~~Suppose the budget owner~~ → Say the budget owner…
- ~~Suppose the study gets funded~~ → Now the study gets funded…
- ~~Suppose a measurement pattern acquires~~ → A measurement pattern acquires…

Also watch "Consider", "Take", "Imagine", "Here is what…" (one is a voice, two is a tic).

## 12. Machine-even rhythm (short posts)

For Coffee & Commute and other short pieces the same habits show up as rhythm: "not X — Y" every few lines, rule-of-three lists, and every paragraph ending on the same punchy fragment. Fix: strip the antitheses and triples, let some sentences run long and a little unpolished, introduce repeated devices differently each time so they don't read as one template stamped five times. Keep the texture (asides, loose grammar, short lines where they earn it).

## 13. Kept on purpose

Read this before cutting anything that looks like a pattern:
- One-line paragraphs at the author's density (Chapter 2, his most unmistakable chapter, ran 13.3 per 1,000 words). Offer the most verdict-like ones as optional cuts: "Direct contact does not scale", "Otherwise the scaffold becomes a cage".
- Jokes that do epistemic work and images that recur as motifs (camel, cow, coffee, Alberto, the tongue test, Reviewer 2).
- Strong claims that then argue with themselves in public.
